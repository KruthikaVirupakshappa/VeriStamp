# Literature Survey: M1 (frequency/noise detector) and RQ1

M1 is a ResNet-50 that receives 4 input channels instead of RGB: a 3-channel high-pass noise residual and a 1-channel FFT log-magnitude spectrum. It is compared with an RGB ResNet-50 baseline under a leave-one-generator-out protocol on GenImage (seen: ADM + VQDM, unseen: Stable Diffusion v1.4).

| # | Paper | Main idea / method | Main takeaway | Link to M1 / RQ1 |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Zhu et al. (2023), **GenImage** (NeurIPS Datasets & Benchmarks) [[arXiv 2306.08571](https://arxiv.org/abs/2306.08571)] | A million-scale benchmark of ImageNet real photos paired with images from 8 generators (Midjourney, SD v1.4, SD v1.5, ADM, GLIDE, Wukong, VQDM, BigGAN). Defines cross-generator and degraded-image evaluation tasks. | Detectors trained on one generator often drop sharply on others, so cross-generator testing is required to measure generalization. | This is M1's dataset and the source of our leave-one-generator-out protocol: train on ADM + VQDM, test on the unseen SD v1.4. |
| 2 | Grommelt et al. (2024), **Fake or JPEG? Revealing Common Biases in Generated Image Detection Datasets** (ECCV Workshops) [[arXiv 2403.17608](https://arxiv.org/abs/2403.17608)] | Shows that in GenImage the real images are JPEG files while the AI images are PNG, and that the two classes have different size distributions. Retrains detectors after removing these biases. | Detectors learn JPEG compression and image size instead of generation artifacts. Removing the biases improves robustness to JPEG and changes cross-generator results. | Directly motivates our equal-JPEG preprocessing (every image re-encoded with the same quality rule and 4:2:0 subsampling) and the native-crop run that removes the resize shortcut found in `m1_run_001`. |
| 3 | Wang et al. (2020), **CNN-generated images are surprisingly easy to spot... for now** (CVPR) [[arXiv 1912.11035](https://arxiv.org/abs/1912.11035)] | Trains a ResNet-50 on ProGAN images versus real images, with blur and JPEG augmentation, and tests it on images from other CNN generators. | A single standard classifier can generalize across many GAN architectures, and augmentation is a large part of why. | The direct template for our RGB ResNet-50 baseline, against which M1 is compared. Its JPEG augmentation also informs our JPEG quality range of 75–95. |
| 4 | Frank et al. (2020), **Leveraging Frequency Analysis for Deep Fake Image Recognition** (ICML) [[arXiv 2003.08685](https://arxiv.org/abs/2003.08685)] | Analyses images in the DCT frequency domain and shows that GAN images have strong, regular spectral artifacts. Simple classifiers trained on the spectrum detect them. | The artifacts come from upsampling layers in the generator, and they are much easier to see in the frequency domain than in pixels. | Main justification for M1's FFT channel: frequency artifacts can be visible even when RGB content looks realistic. |
| 5 | Durall et al. (2020), **Watch your Up-Convolution: CNN Based Generative Deep Neural Networks are Failing to Reproduce Spectral Distributions** (CVPR) [[arXiv 2003.01826](https://arxiv.org/abs/2003.01826)] | Computes a 1-D azimuthally averaged power spectrum and shows that up-convolution generators fail to reproduce the high-frequency spectrum of real images. | Even a very simple classifier on the 1-D spectrum separates real and generated images well. | Supports using a log-magnitude spectrum as an M1 input channel, and suggests a cheap spectral baseline. |
| 6 | Zhang, Karaman & Chang (2019), **Detecting and Simulating Artifacts in GAN Fake Images** (WIFS) [[arXiv 1907.06515](https://arxiv.org/abs/1907.06515)] | Builds AutoGAN, a simulator that reproduces the upsampling artifacts of GANs from real images, and trains a classifier on the image spectrum. | A spectrum-based classifier trained on simulated artifacts can detect GAN images without seeing the target GAN. | Shows that spectrum inputs can generalize to unseen generators, which is the RQ1 hypothesis M1 tests. |
| 7 | Marra et al. (2019), **Do GANs Leave Artificial Fingerprints?** (MIPR) [[arXiv 1812.11842](https://arxiv.org/abs/1812.11842)] | Extracts noise residuals with a denoising filter and averages them over many images of one GAN to obtain a "fingerprint". | Each GAN leaves a stable, model-specific pattern in the noise residual, similar to a camera's sensor noise. | Supports M1's high-pass residual channels. It also warns that residuals may be model-specific, which is a risk for unseen-generator performance. |
| 8 | Fridrich & Kodovsky (2012), **Rich Models for Steganalysis of Digital Images** (IEEE TIFS) [[DOI 10.1109/TIFS.2012.2190402](https://doi.org/10.1109/TIFS.2012.2190402)] | Defines the Spatial Rich Model (SRM): a bank of high-pass filters that suppress image content and keep the noise residual, used to detect hidden data. | Fine-grained statistical changes are easier to detect in high-pass residuals than in raw pixels. | The classical origin of the high-pass filtering that produces M1's 3 residual channels. |
| 9 | Bayar & Stamm (2016), **A Deep Learning Approach to Universal Image Manipulation Detection Using a New Convolutional Layer** (ACM IH&MMSec) [[DOI 10.1145/2909827.2930786](https://doi.org/10.1145/2909827.2930786)] | Adds a constrained convolution as the first layer of a CNN, forcing it to learn prediction-error (high-pass) filters. | CNNs detect manipulations better when the first layer suppresses content and keeps residuals. | A learned alternative to M1's fixed high-pass filter, and it supports adapting ResNet's first convolution (`conv1`) to the new input channels. |
| 10 | Chai et al. (2020), **What Makes Fake Images Detectable? Understanding Properties that Generalize** (ECCV) [[arXiv 2008.10588](https://arxiv.org/abs/2008.10588)] | Uses truncated networks with small receptive fields so that each prediction is made from a local patch, then aggregates patch decisions. | Local texture artifacts generalize better than global image content, and they show which regions give the image away. | Supports training M1 on 224 px crops, and our choice not to resize images, which would blur local artifacts. |
| 11 | Gragnaniello et al. (2021), **Are GAN Generated Images Easy to Detect? A Critical Analysis of the State-of-the-Art** (ICME) [[arXiv 2104.02617](https://arxiv.org/abs/2104.02617)] | Re-tests published detectors under realistic conditions: unseen architectures, JPEG compression and resizing. Proposes a ResNet-50 variant that avoids early down-sampling. | Reported accuracy drops sharply under resizing and compression. Keeping full resolution in the early layers helps. | Directly relevant to the resize shortcut in `m1_run_001` (BigGAN was enlarged, real photos were shrunk) and to our native-crop run. |
| 12 | Corvi et al. (2023), **On the Detection of Synthetic Images Generated by Diffusion Models** (ICASSP) [[arXiv 2211.00680](https://arxiv.org/abs/2211.00680)] | Studies the forensic traces of diffusion models and tests whether detectors built for GANs still work on diffusion images, including after compression and resizing. | Diffusion images leave weaker, different traces from GANs. GAN-trained detectors generalize poorly to them, especially after social-network processing. | Explains why our unseen test is a diffusion model (SD v1.4) and why it is hard. It also motivates training on diffusion generators (ADM, VQDM). |
| 13 | Ricker et al. (2024), **Towards the Detection of Diffusion Model Deepfakes** (VISAPP) [[arXiv 2210.14571](https://arxiv.org/abs/2210.14571)] | Compares the DFT and DCT spectra of GAN and diffusion images, and evaluates detectors on both. | Diffusion models do not show the GAN-style spectral peaks; instead they tend to underestimate high frequencies. | A warning for M1's FFT channel: the clear GAN spectral artifacts may be absent. The useful cue for diffusion images may be missing high-frequency energy. |
| 14 | Tan et al. (2024), **Rethinking the Up-Sampling Operations in CNN-based Generative Network for Generalizable Deepfake Detection (NPR)** (CVPR) [[arXiv 2312.10461](https://arxiv.org/abs/2312.10461)] | Uses Neighboring Pixel Relationships (NPR): the difference between an image and a down-then-up-sampled copy, capturing local upsampling traces. | A simple, low-level upsampling trace generalizes across GANs and diffusion models. | A modern low-level feature similar in spirit to M1's high-pass residual, and a possible extra baseline. |
| 15 | Wang et al. (2023), **DIRE for Diffusion-Generated Image Detection** (ICCV) [[arXiv 2303.09295](https://arxiv.org/abs/2303.09295)] | Inverts and reconstructs each image with a pretrained diffusion model, and classifies the reconstruction error (DIRE). | Diffusion images are reconstructed more accurately than real images, so the error is a strong cue. | A diffusion-specific alternative to M1's hand-designed features. Grommelt et al. (row 2) found JPEG bias in the DIRE benchmark too, so equal-JPEG evaluation matters when comparing with it. |
| 16 | Ojha, Li & Lee (2023), **Towards Universal Fake Image Detectors that Generalize Across Generative Models (UnivFD)** (CVPR) [[arXiv 2302.10174](https://arxiv.org/abs/2302.10174)] | Uses features from a frozen CLIP ViT-L/14 with nearest-neighbour or linear-probe classification instead of training a detector end to end. | A trained classifier over-fits to the artifacts of its training generator; frozen semantic features generalize much better to unseen generators. | The counter-hypothesis to M1 in RQ1. It is the basis for M2 (frozen CLIP + MLP), against which M1's frequency/noise approach is compared. |

## Main gaps this survey shows for M1

1. **Dataset bias (rows 2, 11, 15).** Format, compression and size differences between Real and AI images can be learned instead of generation artifacts. Our control run reproduced this: the resize pipeline gave below-chance results on SD v1.4 when BigGAN was used.
2. **GAN vs diffusion (rows 4–6, 12, 13).** Most frequency evidence comes from GANs. For diffusion models the spectral cue is weaker and different, so the FFT channel is not guaranteed to help.
3. **Residuals may be model-specific (rows 7, 16).** Noise fingerprints differ between generators, which may limit how far M1 generalizes to an unseen generator. This is exactly what RQ1 measures.
