# Literature Survey: M3 (learned watermark) and RQ2 / RQ3

M3 hides a 32-bit message (verdict, confidence, detector ID, image tag and a CRC-8 checksum) in an image with a HiDDeN-style encoder and decoder, using a U-Net encoder and training with real and simulated JPEG and resizing. RQ2 asks whether the stamp survives normal edits better than AI edits. RQ3 asks whether adding the stamp changes the detectors' scores. Authors, titles and arXiv numbers of the rows below were checked on arxiv.org on 23 Nov 2026, except the COCO, SSIM and LPIPS rows.

| # | Paper | Main idea / method | Main takeaway | Link to M3 / RQ2 / RQ3 |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Zhu et al. (2018), **HiDDeN: Hiding Data With Deep Networks** (ECCV) [[arXiv 1807.09937](https://arxiv.org/abs/1807.09937)] | An encoder hides a bit string in an image, a decoder reads it back, and a discriminator keeps the changes hard to see. Noise layers (for example a differentiable JPEG approximation) sit between encoder and decoder. | Putting the distortion in the training loop is what lets a watermark survive it. | The source of M3's networks, losses and JPEG-Mask layer. |
| 2 | Tancik, Mildenhall & Ng (2020), **StegaStamp: Invisible Hyperlinks in Physical Photographs** (CVPR) [[arXiv 1904.05343](https://arxiv.org/abs/1904.05343)] | A U-Net encoder hides a message that survives printing and photographing. | A U-Net with skip connections hides messages more reliably than a plain stack of convolutions. | The inspiration for M3's U-Net encoder: bit accuracy rose from 92.97% (conv) to 99.66% (U-Net). |
| 3 | Jia, Fang & Zhang (2021), **MBRS: Enhancing Robustness of DNN-based Watermarking by Mini-Batch of Real and Simulated JPEG Compression** (ACM MM) [[arXiv 2108.08211](https://arxiv.org/abs/2108.08211)] | Alternates mini-batches of real JPEG and a simulated JPEG during training. | Real JPEG has no gradient, so mixing it with a differentiable approximation improves robustness. | Stage 3 of M3 mixes real JPEG-50, JPEG-Mask, resizing and no distortion. |
| 4 | Fernandez, Couairon, Jégou, Douze & Furon (2023), **The Stable Signature: Rooting Watermarks in Latent Diffusion Models** [[arXiv 2303.15435](https://arxiv.org/abs/2303.15435)] | Combines watermarking with latent diffusion models so that the generator's outputs carry a watermark. | The watermark is built into the generator, so it only marks images from a model the owner controls. | M3 differs: it stamps any image, real or generated, after detection. |
| 5 | Wen, Kirchenbauer, Geiping & Goldstein (2023), **Tree-Ring Watermarks: Fingerprints for Diffusion Images that are Invisible and Robust** [[arXiv 2305.20030](https://arxiv.org/abs/2305.20030)] | Watermarks the outputs of diffusion models through the initial noise. | Another generator-side watermark, tied to the model that makes the image. | Contrast with M3's post-hoc stamp. |
| 6 | Zhao et al. (2023, revised 2024), **Invisible Image Watermarks Are Provably Removable Using Generative AI** [[arXiv 2306.01953](https://arxiv.org/abs/2306.01953)] | Shows that invisible watermarks can be removed with generative models. | Regeneration attacks defeat invisible pixel watermarks. | Matches our RQ2 result: after a Stable Diffusion VAE round trip only 13.6% of stamps stay valid. |
| 7 | Saberi et al. (2023, revised 2024), **Robustness of AI-Image Detectors: Fundamental Limits and Practical Attacks** [[arXiv 2310.00076](https://arxiv.org/abs/2310.00076)] | Studies attacks on AI-image detectors and watermarks. | Both detectors and low-perturbation watermarks can be attacked. | Supports our point that a missing stamp is not proof of regeneration. |
| 8 | Bui, Agarwal & Collomosse (2023), **TrustMark: Universal Watermarking for Arbitrary Resolution Images** [[arXiv 2311.18297](https://arxiv.org/abs/2311.18297)] | A GAN-based watermark with spatio-spectral losses and a resolution-scaling method for images of any size. Reports a PSNR above 40 dB. | Arbitrary-resolution watermarking is a recognised problem. | M3 solves the same problem more simply (stamp at 128 px, enlarge the residual), with lower image quality (31.5 dB). The two were measured on different data. |
| 9 | Gowal et al. (2025), **SynthID-Image: Image watermarking at internet scale** [[arXiv 2510.09263](https://arxiv.org/abs/2510.09263)] | A post-hoc, model-independent invisible watermark that Google reports using on over ten billion images and video frames. | An industrial post-hoc watermark; the authors note robustness and security are imperfect in practice. | The closest deployed idea to M3. M3 stores a verdict with a checksum and is not signed or deployed. |
| 10 | Coalition for Content Provenance and Authenticity, **C2PA Content Credentials: Technical Specification 2.2** [[spec](https://c2pa.org/specifications/specifications/2.2/specs/C2PA_Specification.html)], and the Content Authenticity Initiative, **The three pillars of provenance that make up durable Content Credentials** [[blog](https://contentauthenticity.org/blog/three-pillars-of-provenance)] | Signed provenance data attached to a file. Platforms and CDNs often strip it; the CAI describes adding an invisible watermark so a stripped image can be matched back to its record. | Metadata alone is fragile. | The motivation for M3. M3 is the watermark part of this idea, but its stamp is not signed. |
| 11 | Guo et al. (2024), **AI-generated Image Detection: Passive or Watermark?** [[arXiv 2411.13553](https://arxiv.org/abs/2411.13553)] | ImageDetectBench compares passive detectors with watermark-based detectors (watermarks embedded at generation time) on effectiveness, robustness and efficiency. | Watermark-based detectors consistently outperform passive ones on this benchmark. | Related to RQ3, but it compares the two kinds of detector. It does not measure how an added watermark changes a passive detector's scores. |
| 12 | Lin et al. (2014), **Microsoft COCO: Common Objects in Context** [[arXiv 1405.0312](https://arxiv.org/abs/1405.0312)] | A large dataset of everyday photos. | A standard source of natural photos. | M3's training, validation and test photos (10,000 / 500 / 500). |
| 13 | Wang et al. (2004), **Image quality assessment: from error visibility to structural similarity** (IEEE TIP); Zhang et al. (2018), **The Unreasonable Effectiveness of Deep Features as a Perceptual Metric (LPIPS)** [[arXiv 1801.03924](https://arxiv.org/abs/1801.03924)] | A structural similarity index and a learned perceptual distance. | PSNR alone does not capture how visible a change is. | The image-quality metrics in our evaluation (PSNR, SSIM, LPIPS). |

## Main gaps this survey shows for M3
- **RQ3 is not covered.** We did not find a study of how adding an invisible watermark changes the scores of trained passive detectors on the same image. The closest, Guo et al., compares watermark-based with passive detection.
- **Image quality.** A published arbitrary-resolution method (TrustMark) reports higher image quality than M3's enlarged-residual approach, although the benchmarks differ.
- **Trust.** C2PA signs its claims, while M3's stamp is unsigned, so a valid stamp only records what our detectors said.
- **Regeneration.** Published attacks, and our own RQ2 result, show that regeneration removes invisible pixel watermarks.
